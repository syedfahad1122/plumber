Water damage risks in Tampa

Tampa's water damage comes from three directions: storm surge pushed up Tampa Bay, heavy rain that overwhelms rivers and street drainage, and the everyday plumbing, roof and AC leaks that happen in any Florida home. The 2024 hurricane season showed how quickly the first two can arrive one after the other.

Storm surge: what Hurricane Helene showed

When Hurricane Helene passed offshore on September 26, 2024, the National Weather Service recorded about 7.2 feet of surge at East Bay and 6.8 feet at Old Port Tampa. Low-lying neighborhoods flooded overnight: Davis Islands streets went under water, floodwater surrounded Tampa General Hospital, and homes in Palmetto Beach and along the Alafia River were inundated (Axios Tampa Bay). Saltwater surge is treated as contaminated (Category 3) water, so wet drywall, insulation and carpet usually have to be removed, not just dried.

Rain and river flooding: what Hurricane Milton showed

Two weeks later, Hurricane Milton made landfall near Siesta Key on October 9, 2024 and dropped 10 to 20 inches of rain across Tampa Bay, including 11.73 inches at Tampa International Airport and 15.66 inches in Temple Terrace. The Hillsborough River reached a record crest of 38.16 feet at Morris Bridge on October 12, flooding homes days after the storm had passed (Spectrum Bay News 9). If you live near the river, its tributaries or a retention pond, keep watching water levels after the rain stops.

Everyday leaks

Most calls are not hurricanes. Air conditioners run most of the year in Tampa, and algae-clogged AC condensate drains are a constant source of ceiling and closet leaks. Older homes in the city's established neighborhoods may still have galvanized steel or other aging supply lines, and homes built roughly from the late 1970s to the mid-1990s may have polybutylene pipe, which is prone to failure. If you don't know what your pipes are made of, ask a licensed plumber to check during any leak repair.

Tampa-specific steps after water damage

  1. Close your private shut-off valve. In the City of Tampa, the curb stop at the meter belongs to the City, and only authorized City staff may operate it. Your own shut-off valve is usually on the side of the house, between the meter box and the home. If you don't have one, the City can schedule a turn-off (Tampa Water FAQ).
  2. Report leaks on the City side. For a leak at the meter, a hydrant, a water main or a service outage, call the City of Tampa Utilities Call Center at (813) 274-8811. Emergency calls are answered 24/7.
  3. Check your evacuation zone before storm season. Hillsborough County changed its evacuation zones for 2026: Zone A expanded in places, some parcels moved from Zone D to C, and some areas were added to Zone E. Look up your address at HCFL.gov/HEAT and sign up for alerts at HCFL.gov/Alert (Hillsborough County).
  4. Know which repairs need a permit. After a declared disaster, the City of Tampa's post-hurricane permitting guide says a drywall flood cut of two feet or less and standalone finish work (flooring, cabinets, countertops) need no permit. Electrical work, roof repairs over 500 square feet, and finish work combined with plumbing or electrical work do need one. Homes in a Special Flood Hazard Area also need a Substantial Improvement Determination form even when no permit is required. Confirm current rules with Construction Services at (813) 274-3100, option 1, or through Accela (City of Tampa permitting guide).
  5. Document before anything is removed. Take photos and video of every wet room, plus the water line on the walls, before cleanup starts. Your insurance claim depends on it, and standard homeowners policies usually exclude flood water, which is covered separately by flood insurance.

Rules and phone numbers change, so check the linked official pages for the latest. Unincorporated Hillsborough County, Temple Terrace and Plant City have their own utilities and permitting offices.

What to do while you wait

  1. Shut off water at the main valve or the leaking appliance.
  2. Turn off power to wet areas at the breaker.
  3. Move valuables and furniture out of the water.
  4. Photograph the damage for your insurer.

Tampa water damage questions

Who do I call about a leak at my water meter in Tampa?

If the leak is at the meter, a hydrant or a water main, call the City of Tampa Utilities Call Center at (813) 274-8811, which answers emergency calls 24/7. Leaks on your side of the meter (inside the walls, under the slab, at fixtures) are a job for a licensed plumber.

Do I need a permit to replace wet drywall in Tampa?

After a declared disaster, the City of Tampa's guide says a flood cut of two feet or less from the floor needs no permit, but electrical work does, and finish work combined with plumbing or electrical work does too. Homes in a Special Flood Hazard Area need a Substantial Improvement Determination form. Check current rules with Construction Services at (813) 274-3100.

Is storm surge damage covered by homeowners insurance?

Usually not. Standard homeowners policies generally exclude flood and storm surge, which are covered by a separate flood policy (NFIP or private). Wind-driven rain through a damaged roof is often handled differently. Read your policy and ask your insurer or agent.

How fast can someone get to my home in Tampa?

Arrival times depend on the company and on demand, especially after storms. When you call, ask the Tampa provider for their expected arrival time before you agree to service.

Do restoration companies in Tampa work with insurance?

Many do. Ask the company how they work with your insurer, and check your own policy for what's covered.

What does water damage cleanup cost in Tampa?

It depends on the size of the wet area, the type of water and what has to be removed. Use our calculator for a rough range, then get a written estimate from the provider.

Yes. Calling is free and there's no obligation to hire the company you're connected with.
